How to race

You are the cox

The race

  • Venues: pick the course on the race card: SIRC (nine lanes, the island grandstand, podium and 750 m bridge), Lake Karapiro (the Domain bank, with the dam past the finish), Lake Barrington (a narrow lake in a forested Tasmanian gorge, where a gale always blows: a hard cross-tail or cross-head that swings the splits and pushes you off your line) or the old Head of the River course on the Nepean, finishing by the Penrith bridges. The other three have eight lanes.
  • Boat class (race card): the eight, a coxed four, a single scull (no cox: you set the rate and steer yourself) or, at Lake Barrington only, the old coxed quad with big back arms on its riggers. Each class keeps its own crew. Edward McDonald and those back arms do not get on.
  • Online mode (top of the boatshed) is separate from everything else: it has its own crew, cox, school and boat, its own win record, and the host's course never changes anyone's race card. You're only online while you're in it. Put your name in and pick Host a race: you get an invite code for each friend to send them (with the link to the game), and they send you back a reply code to paste in. That connects your browsers straight to each other, so your friends don't need a Claude account. If you're all signed in to Claude and the game's been shared with you, you can also create a race with a four-letter code, or join one from Open races. The host picks the venue, distance, boat class, conditions and whether GPS crews fill the empty lanes. Everyone races at once on the same course in the same weather; your clock starts at your own green light, so a slow connection never costs you, and a false start just means the starter goes again for you. Infinite energy is on for everyone unless the host turns it off. No coach bonuses, and online times don't count as PBs or win medals. Direct connections can fail on some work, school or public Wi-Fi; mobile data usually works.
  • Course preview: before each race the camera flies from the finish line back to the start, past the venue's landmarks. Space or a click skips it.
  • Start: wait for the green light, then hit Space. Go early and you get a yellow card. Two and you're out.
  • The slider: the yellow marker is the blade. Hit Space as it reaches the catch (blade in) and again at the finish (blade out). The blades only go in when you hit the catch, so miss it (or don't press) and that whole stroke is lost. Clean timing means more run; a late finish can catch a crab.
  • Racing start: three-quarter, half, three-quarter, three-quarter, then full slide. The catch end moves up the slide for the short strokes.
  • Row every stroke (option in the boatshed): time the catch and finish on every stroke of the race. Good timing beats the auto crew; every catch you miss is a stroke the boat doesn't get.
  • Rate is speed, and speed costs energy. Your tank is filled so a crew can hold 35 strokes a minute from the start to the line. Rate higher and it drains faster, all the way up to the maximum of 60. Under 32 you recover slowly.
  • Run the tank dry and the crew blows up: the rate collapses to 28 and you go backwards through the field.
  • Shift down to about 35 after the start, save a little, and empty the tank in the sprint.
  • Your cox's ability shows under the energy bar. Gandhi earns energy for every crew you pass, Gooby's fun fact (F) is a gamble, Porchy's junkyard dog (F) holds 40 for a minute, and so on. Energy above 100% shows as a gold band on the bar. With voices on, the cox calls the race out loud and the crew shout over the top of him.
  • Power 10: extra pressure for ten strokes. Nail the catch and finish on each one for free speed and swing afterwards.
  • Wind: a tailwind makes the splits much quicker and a headwind much slower. A crosswind pushes the boat sideways and swings the bow, so keep steering. The arrow by your lane shows where it's pushing you.
  • Buoys: every separate crash into them is a 50/50 chance of catching a crab, and blades on the buoys slow you. Leave your lane and the umpire warns you. Too long out of it and you're excluded. Every bit of rudder costs a little speed.
  • Collisions: steer into another crew and your blades clash first (both crews slow down and might catch a crab), then the riggers crash: both boats bounce apart, lose a lot of speed and probably catch a crab, and one crab in four throws someone out. Online, the umpire only waves the white flag when you leave your lane (no exclusions), so you can go and crash into your friends.
  • Crabs happen at very high rates (the risk starts to climb above 42), when cooked, or in rough water. Hit Space fast to recover. One crab in four is an ejector: the handle throws him out of the boat and the crew rows the rest of the race without him.
  • Infinite energy (option in the boatshed): the tank never empties and there's no rate limit, so wind it up past 100 and see how fast an eight can go. These races don't earn medals or PBs.
  • Medals and coaches: every win earns 5 medals (10 for a 2000 m win). Spend them in the boatshed to hire better coaches, in order: Nigel, Andrew Rowley, Hoffman, Jack, then JB. Each one makes the crew use a little less energy.
  • Your boat: the Hudson, Empacher and Filippi only change the look. The old Shore Association boat has a 50% chance that a rigger or footplate breaks mid-race: that rower is out for the rest of it, and the boat pulls toward his side.

Pacing rule of thumb

35 is the baseline every crew's tank can hold for the whole course, at any distance. A normal start at 44 then 35 leaves a little for the sprint. Turn on the pace guide to see the rate your tank can afford from here to the line.
